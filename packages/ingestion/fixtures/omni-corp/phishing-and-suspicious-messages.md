# Omni Corp Phishing and Suspicious Messages

Document owner: Security Operations. Last reviewed: 2026-09-12.

Phishing is the most common way attackers reach Omni Corp. Everyone is expected to recognise it and report it quickly.

## Common signs

- Urgency or threats: "your account will be closed today".
- A request for a password, MFA code, gift cards or a payment change.
- A sender name you recognise with an address that does not end in `omnicorp.example`.
- Links whose real address differs from the text when you hover over them.
- Unexpected attachments, especially archives, macro-enabled Office files and HTML files.
- A "shared document" notice you were not expecting.

Omni Corp IT will never ask for your password or MFA code, and will never send a link asking you to sign in to a mailbox quota page.

## How to report

1. In Outlook, use the **Report Phish** button in the ribbon. This sends it to Security and removes it from your mailbox.
2. If the button is missing, forward the message **as an attachment** to `security@omnicorp.example`. Forwarding as an attachment keeps the headers Security needs.
3. If you clicked a link or opened an attachment, say so in `#it-incidents` right away. Fast reports limit damage and are never penalised.
4. If you entered your password, change it immediately at https://id.omnicorp.example/reset.

## What happens next

Security checks the message, removes copies from other mailboxes and blocks the sender and links. You get a short reply, usually within one business day, saying whether it was malicious.

## Phishing simulations

Security sends simulated phishing emails every month. Clicking one leads to a short training page, not a penalty. Repeated clicks lead to a conversation with Security about extra training.

## Voice and chat scams

Attackers also call, text, or message on Slack and Teams pretending to be IT or an executive. Hang up and call the Service Desk number from the intranet, or verify through a different channel. Payment or bank detail changes must be verified by phone with a known contact before action.

## Gift-card and payment requests

A message from an "executive" asking you to buy gift cards or urgently pay an invoice is fraud. Report it and do not reply.
